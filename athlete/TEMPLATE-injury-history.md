# Injury History and Current State

> **Read before any programming. No exceptions.** (CLAUDE.md §0.2)
>
> "Healed" is not "irrelevant." A resolved injury changes what the plan should look like
> permanently — it sets the movements that get screened, the loads that get earned rather
> than assumed, and the early warnings that get taken seriously instead of trained through.

> **Filled during intake, not before** — `skills/intake` covers it in its own conversation,
> because it takes one. If intake genuinely surfaces no injury history, **write that down
> and stop**. Do not invent a programming constraint that does not exist: an imagined bad
> knee is as damaging to a plan as a forgotten real one, and harder to argue with later.
>
> Delete this box and the guidance italics as you fill each section in.

## <Site> — <one line: what state it is in now>

Repeat this block per site. Name the site the way the athlete names it. A site with a history
gets its own section even when it is currently fine; that is the whole point of the file.

**Status:** _healed and stable · managed · currently symptomatic · undiagnosed. Say which._

- Nature of the original injury: _what it actually was, including the diagnosis if there is one._
- When: _year is usually enough._
- Side: _left / right / bilateral / n-a. Ask; do not infer it from anything else in the chart._
- How it resolved, or why it hasn't: _what was actually done, and how long it took._
- Imaging or assessment on file: _what exists, when, and with whom. "None" is an answer._
- Last flare: _when, and — this is the part that matters — **what set it off**._
- **Early warning signs that precede a flare, IN THE ATHLETE'S OWN WORDS:**
  1. _Quote them. Do not paraphrase into clinical language._
  2. _People who have lived with a site can usually distinguish "this is the bad kind" from
     ordinary soreness. That distinction is the single most useful thing in this file and it
     does not survive being tidied up._
- What reliably aggravates it: _movements, loads, positions, durations, surfaces._
- What reliably helps: _if they already have a routine that works, write it out in full. A
  proven tool is worth more than anything the coach would otherwise propose._
- Movement permanently off the table: _"nothing" is a valid answer. So is "not without a
  conversation" — record which._

**Pain pattern — during, after, and the next morning.** _All three, separately. A site that is
quiet during the session and angry the next morning is a different problem from one that hurts
under load, and the distinction changes the prescription rather than just the volume._

**Standing programming rules for this site:** _what the coach must do every time, not advice._

**Prehab / maintenance work that stays in the program regardless of phase:** _if any._

## The rules that apply to all of it

These are the system's, not this athlete's. They hold on every chart that has this file.

1. **Work around, never through.** Modify range, then load, then pattern — in that order.
2. **Pain that changes gait or movement pattern stops the session** and gets referred out
   (CLAUDE.md §5.2). That is a floor, not a judgement call, and it is not negotiable by the
   athlete.
3. **Two consecutive substitutions out of the same pattern is a signal, not a preference.**
   Log it and investigate.
4. **A resolved injury during an energy deficit deserves more caution, not less.** Reduced
   recovery capacity and reduced tissue tolerance travel together, and a deficit is exactly
   the phase where old sites reappear.
5. **This file is not medical advice and the coach is not a physiotherapist.** For anything
   active, unclear, or worsening: get assessed. Record the referral here rather than
   programming around it.

## The bright line

_What would make the coach stop programming this site and refer out today, stated concretely
enough that both of you would recognise it. Locking, giving way, loss of function, inability to
bear weight, numbness — plus anything this athlete has personally learned to treat as serious.
Written down in advance, because it is not a judgement anyone makes well mid-session._
