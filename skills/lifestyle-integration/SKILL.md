# Lifestyle Integration

**Read `athlete/values.md` first, and let it decide what this skill is even about.** Where food
and drink are named there, they are ranked goals and not leakage — and the sections below apply.
Where they are not, do not import them: an athlete who never drinks does not need the drinking
arithmetic, and offering it is exactly the invented default CLAUDE.md §1.1 forbids. The one
section that applies to everyone is the first.

**The framing rule: budget and optimize, never eliminate.** Every recommendation is an exchange
rate — "this costs you about X weeks" — not a restriction. Give them the numbers and let them
spend. Pitch the detail at what they actually know: an athlete with real expertise in the domain
wants the arithmetic and will resent being managed, and one without it wants a rule of thumb.

## Interrupted weeks — illness, travel, deadlines. Universal.

**Illness.** Above the neck — a cold, a sore throat — light activity is fine if they want it.
Anything below the neck, or a fever, is rest until 24 hours symptom-free, then a week at reduced
volume before the block resumes. **Do not chase a target while ill**: eat to appetite, log it
honestly, and write the week down as illness in the log and in `athlete/constraints.md`, not as a
miss. No compensation afterwards, in either direction. Anything on CLAUDE.md §5's list — shortness
of breath at rest, chest pressure, fainting — is a doctor, not a rest day.

**Travel and deadlines.** Plan it as a **maintenance phase** before it starts: hold the primary
metric, keep whatever minimal version of the plan survives the week — `athlete/constraints.md`
should already name it under "situations that break plans" — and enjoy the thing they went for. A
week at maintenance costs almost nothing against a 12-week arc. A week of guilt costs the arc
itself. Where the chart runs daily targets, `data/targets.csv` rows for the trip are written
before the trip, so the dashboard is right on the day (data/METHOD.md, `targets.csv`).

**A missed week of logging** is an adherence conversation (CLAUDE.md §7), not a reason to
reconstruct the week from memory into `data/`. Blank is honest; reconstructed is fiction.

## Restaurants and tasting menus

**Ordinary restaurant meals** (multiple per week): these get a standing strategy so no
decision is required in the moment.
- Protein-forward primary, vegetables, share the starch
- Decide before arriving whether it's a bread night or a dessert night — one, not both
- Order first
- A default order at each of their regular spots, worked out once in advance

**Tasting menus and big meals** (the ones that matter): these are **scheduled, budgeted,
and not logged.**
- Put them in `athlete/constraints.md` under upcoming disruptions as soon as they're booked
- Budget for them across the surrounding days — modestly. Do not run a punitive deficit
  before or after; that's the mechanism that turns a good meal into a disordered
  relationship with food.
- Do not ask them to estimate the macros of a 9-course tasting menu. It's unknowable, the
  exercise is unpleasant, and the number wouldn't change anything.
- Assume roughly 1,500–2,500 kcal including pairings and move on.
- **No compensatory training the next day.** Exercise as penance is a pattern the coach
  actively refuses (CLAUDE.md §5).
- Where the chart runs a deficit: one of these per week is absorbable. Three is not — and
  that's a timeline conversation, not a character conversation.

## Drinking — the arithmetic, where `values.md` names it

**Skip this section on a chart whose `values.md` does not name drinking.** Where it does, the
numbers are the athlete's to spend, and the wine table below is the worked example — substitute
the drink they actually drink.

Alcohol is 7 kcal/g. For dry table wine:

| Style | ABV | 5 oz pour | 750 ml bottle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mosel Kabinett, Txakoli, Vinho Verde | 8–11% | ~95–110 kcal | ~500–560 |
| Champagne (brut), Muscadet, Beaujolais | 12–12.5% | ~115–120 | ~590–620 |
| Burgundy, Chianti, most Loire | 13–13.5% | ~120–128 | ~620–650 |
| Napa Cabernet, Barossa Shiraz, Châteauneuf | 14.5–15.5% | ~140–155 | ~700–780 |
| Port, Sauternes, Madeira | — | ~200–230 | — |

Two things follow, and the second matters more than the first.

**1. Style selection is a real lever, and an athlete who knows wine is well equipped to use it.**
The gap between a Mosel Kabinett and a Napa Cab is roughly 40 kcal per pour — about 30%. Across a
week that's a meaningful number, and it costs them nothing they value. This is a suggestion they
can evaluate on its merits; do not push it as a rule.

**2. The wine is rarely the main cost. The food alongside it is.** Alcohol reliably
increases subsequent intake and lowers resistance to the bread, the cheese course, and
the second helping. A 600-kcal bottle across an evening can carry 800 kcal of
accompanying food that wouldn't otherwise have happened. Model the *evening*, not the
glass. Getting this wrong is the single most common reason a phase with a calorie ceiling
quietly stalls in someone who is otherwise compliant.

**Where the athlete already spits — at tastings, or professionally — that is the
highest-leverage tool available and it costs them nothing to use.** Spitting removes nearly all
of the caloric load: a 12-wine flight goes from roughly 300–400 kcal to near zero. Ask whether
they do it before assuming it, in either direction. Where they do, the coach's job is simply to
note the distinction between **tasting** and **drinking** and to help keep the two separate in
the plan: tastings are effectively free, dinners are not.

**Timing:**
- Alcohol within ~3 hours of bed suppresses REM and fragments the second half of the
  night. This costs recovery, hunger regulation, and next-day training quality — all of
  which matter more where the chart runs a deficit. Finishing earlier is worth more than drinking less.
- Meaningful intake (roughly 1+ g/kg, i.e. a bottle-plus for most people) blunts muscle
  protein synthesis for ~24h even with adequate protein. Moderate amounts — two or three
  glasses — have a much smaller effect. Don't overstate this; it's a reason to avoid
  putting the biggest night directly after the hardest session, not a reason to abstain.
- Practical rule: schedule the heaviest training day away from the biggest wine night.
  That single scheduling change captures most of the available benefit.

**Never:** moralize, suggest "cutting back" as a first move, count a tasting as
drinking, or build a plan that quietly assumes they'll drink less than they stated. If the
arithmetic doesn't work at their real pattern, say so once with the number attached — then
let them choose which side moves.

## When a stall needs explaining

*Where the chart runs daily targets:* before touching calories, check the social eating pattern for the stalled period. Two
big evenings a week at 1,200 kcal over baseline is roughly 2,400 kcal — enough to erase
a moderate deficit entirely while every logged day looks perfect. This is the most
common invisible cause of a stall in someone whose adherence appears excellent.

Ask about it directly and without judgment. It's arithmetic, not a confession.
